Symbols

1. Severity Distributions & Moments

Raw & central moments

$$E[X]=\int_0^\infty x f(x)\,dx,\qquad E[X^k]=\int_0^\infty x^k f(x)\,dx$$ $$\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2,\quad \mathrm{CV}=\frac{\sigma_X}{E[X]},\quad \text{skew}=\frac{E[(X-\mu)^3]}{\sigma^3}$$

Survival form (nonneg $X$): $\;E[X]=\int_0^\infty S(x)\,dx,\quad E[X^k]=\int_0^\infty k x^{k-1}S(x)\,dx.$

3. Aggregate / Compound Models

$S=X_1+\cdots+X_N$ (i.i.d. $X_i$ independent of $N$).

Compound moments

$$E[S]=E[N]\,E[X]$$ $$\mathrm{Var}(S)=E[N]\,\mathrm{Var}(X)+\mathrm{Var}(N)\,(E[X])^2$$

Compound Poisson ($N\sim$ Poisson $\lambda$): $\;E[S]=\lambda E[X],\;\mathrm{Var}(S)=\lambda E[X^2].$

PGF/MGF: $P_S(z)=P_N(P_X(z))$, $\;M_S(t)=P_N(M_X(t))$.

Conditional (mixing) variance

$$\mathrm{Var}(S)=E[\mathrm{Var}(S\mid\Theta)]+\mathrm{Var}(E[S\mid\Theta])$$

(Total variance / "law of total variance" — also basis for Bühlmann.)

Convolution — the official FAM route to $\Pr(S=s)$

Discrete severity on $0,1,2,\dots$ with pmf $f_X$. The $n$-fold convolution $f_X^{*n}$ is the pmf of $X_1+\cdots+X_n$:

$$f_X^{*n}(x)=\sum_{y}f_X^{*(n-1)}(x-y)\,f_X(y),\qquad f_X^{*0}(0)=1,\quad f_X^{*1}=f_X$$ $$f_S(s)=\Pr(S=s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Pr(N=n)\,f_X^{*n}(s)$$ Two-variable form (the one you actually compute by hand) $$\Pr(X_1+X_2=s)=\sum_{y=0}^{s}f_X(y)\,f_X(s-y)$$

If $f_X(0)=0$ then $f_X^{*n}(s)=0$ for $n>s$, so the outer sum is finite — usually just $n=0,1,2,3$.

Count the orderings

Convolution sums over ordered pairs: claims of sizes $1$ and $2$ contribute $2f_X(1)f_X(2)$, not $f_X(1)f_X(2)$.

Normal & lognormal approximation to $S$

Normal — match $E[S]$ and $\mathrm{Var}(S)$; $\mu=E[S],\ \sigma=\mathrm{SD}(S)$ $$\Pr(S\le s)\approx\Phi\!\Big(\frac{s-\mu}{\sigma}\Big)$$ Continuity correction — only when $S$ is discrete (frequency and severity discrete) $$\Pr(S\le s)\approx\Phi\!\Big(\frac{s+0.5-\mu}{\sigma}\Big),\qquad \Pr(S\ge s)\approx1-\Phi\!\Big(\frac{s-0.5-\mu}{\sigma}\Big)$$

Direction rule: shift so the point $s$ lands inside the event. $\Pr(S\le s)$ and $\Pr(S>s)$ both use $s+0.5$; $\Pr(S\ge s)$ and $\Pr(S\lt s)$ both use $s-0.5$. With spread-out support (e.g. $200,400,1000$) use the midpoints between adjacent possible values, not $\pm0.5$.

Lognormal — moment-match to $m_1=E[S]$, $m_2=E[S^2]$ $$\sigma_L^2=\ln\!\Big(\frac{m_2}{m_1^2}\Big),\qquad \mu_L=\ln m_1-\tfrac12\sigma_L^2$$ $$\Pr(S\le s)\approx\Phi\!\Big(\frac{\ln s-\mu_L}{\sigma_L}\Big)$$

Captures right skew, so it beats the normal in the tail. No continuity correction (it is used for continuous-ish aggregates).

Stop-loss premium under the approximations $$\textbf{Normal:}\quad E[(S-d)_+]\approx\sigma\,\phi\!\Big(\frac{d-\mu}{\sigma}\Big)-(d-\mu)\Big[1-\Phi\!\Big(\frac{d-\mu}{\sigma}\Big)\Big]$$ $$\textbf{Lognormal:}\quad E[(S-d)_+]\approx e^{\mu_L+\sigma_L^2/2}\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{\mu_L+\sigma_L^2-\ln d}{\sigma_L}\Big)-d\Big[1-\Phi\!\Big(\frac{\ln d-\mu_L}{\sigma_L}\Big)\Big]$$

Panjer recursion (discrete $X$ on $0,1,2,\dots$; $N$ is $(a,b,0/1)$) — ASTAM background, not FAM

Not a FAM outcome. The syllabus names the convolution method and the normal/lognormal approximation (both above) as the ways to get aggregate probabilities. Panjer is efficient and worth recognizing, but spend your memorization budget on the two blocks above.

$$f_S(s)=\frac{[p_1-(a+b)p_0]f_X(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\big(a+\tfrac{bj}{s}\big)f_X(j)f_S(s-j)}{1-a f_X(0)}$$

$(a,b,0)$ start: $f_S(0)=P_N(f_X(0))$; $(a,b,0)$ recursion simplifies to $f_S(s)=\dfrac{1}{1-af_X(0)}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\big(a+\tfrac{bj}{s}\big)f_X(j)f_S(s-j)$.

Individual risk model

$S=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ (fixed $n$, indep, not identical): $E[S]=\sum E[X_i]$, $\mathrm{Var}(S)=\sum\mathrm{Var}(X_i)$.

4. Stop-Loss & Limited Expected Value

Limited expected value (LEV) — payment under limit $d$

$$E[X\wedge d]=\int_0^d S(x)\,dx=\int_0^d x f(x)dx + d\,S(d)$$ $$E[(X\wedge d)^2]=\int_0^d 2x\,S(x)\,dx$$

Stop-loss / excess

$$E[(X-d)_+]=\int_d^\infty S(x)\,dx=E[X]-E[X\wedge d]$$ $$E[(X-d)_+]=\int_d^\infty (x-d)f(x)\,dx$$

Net stop-loss premium for aggregate: $E[(S-d)_+]=E[S]-E[S\wedge d]$.

Recursion (discrete, step $h$): $E[(S-(d+h))_+]=E[(S-d)_+]-h\,[1-F_S(d)]$.

Mean residual life (mean excess): $\;e(d)=E[X-d\mid X>d]=\dfrac{E[(X-d)_+]}{S(d)}.$

Mean-excess $e(d)$ shortcuts (memorize)

For these distributions $X-d\mid X\gt d$ has a clean form, so $e(d)$ is closed-form:

Dist of $X$$X-d\mid X\gt d$ is…$e(d)$
Exponential($\theta$)Exponential($\theta$) (memoryless)$\theta$
Uniform$(0,b)$, $d\lt b$Uniform$(0,b-d)$$\dfrac{b-d}{2}$
Pareto($\alpha,\theta$)Pareto($\alpha,\theta+d$)$\dfrac{\theta+d}{\alpha-1}\;(\alpha\gt1)$
Single-param Pareto($\alpha,\theta$), $d\gt\theta$—$\dfrac{d}{\alpha-1}\;(\alpha\gt1)$

5. Risk Measures (VaR & TVaR)

What a risk measure does

A risk measure $\rho(X)$ maps a loss $X$ to a single number that quantifies its risk — used for solvency/capital. The two on the exam are Value-at-Risk (a quantile) and Tail-Value-at-Risk (the average loss beyond that quantile).

Value-at-Risk (VaR)

$$\mathrm{VaR}_p(X)=\pi_p=F_X^{-1}(p)\qquad(\text{the }100p\text{th percentile}).$$

It is the loss exceeded with probability $1-p$. The exam table gives $\mathrm{VaR}_p$ (percentile) for most distributions, so you rarely invert the cdf by hand.

Lognormal: $\;\mathrm{VaR}_p(X)=e^{\mu+\sigma z_p}$, where $z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)$.

Tail-Value-at-Risk (TVaR / CTE)

Definition & mean-excess shortcut $$\mathrm{TVaR}_p(X)=E[X\mid X\gt \mathrm{VaR}_p(X)]=\mathrm{VaR}_p(X)+e\big(\mathrm{VaR}_p(X)\big)$$ $$\mathrm{TVaR}_p(X)=\mathrm{VaR}_p(X)+\frac{E[X]-E[X\wedge \mathrm{VaR}_p(X)]}{1-p}=\frac{1}{1-p}\int_p^1 \mathrm{VaR}_u(X)\,du.$$

The mean-excess form (using the $e(d)$ table above) avoids integration when the table gives $\mathrm{VaR}_p$. Always $\mathrm{TVaR}_p\ge\mathrm{VaR}_p$.

Closed forms — Normal & Lognormal (not all on the exam table) $$\textbf{Normal}(\mu,\sigma^2):\quad \mathrm{TVaR}_p(X)=\mu+\sigma\,\frac{\phi(z_p)}{1-p},\qquad \phi(z)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}.$$ $$\textbf{Lognormal}(\mu,\sigma):\quad \mathrm{TVaR}_p(X)=e^{\mu+\sigma^2/2}\,\frac{\Phi(\sigma-z_p)}{1-p}=E[X]\cdot\frac{\Phi(\sigma-z_p)}{1-p}.$$

Here $z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)$; $\phi$ is the standard-normal pdf, $\Phi$ its cdf. The lognormal form needs only $E[X]$ and $z_p$ — no $\mathrm{VaR}_p$.

Coherence

$\rho$ is coherent if it satisfies all four properties. VaR fails subadditivity (so it can penalize diversification); TVaR satisfies all four.

PropertyDefinition$\mathrm{VaR}_p$$\mathrm{TVaR}_p$
Translation invariance$\rho(X+c)=\rho(X)+c$✓✓
Positive homogeneity$\rho(cX)=c\,\rho(X),\;c\gt0$✓✓
Subadditivity$\rho(X+Y)\le\rho(X)+\rho(Y)$✗✓
Monotonicity$\Pr(X\le Y)=1\Rightarrow\rho(X)\le\rho(Y)$✓✓
Coherent?NoYes
VaR is not coherent

VaR violates subadditivity only. Counterexample: i.i.d. $X,Y$ with $\Pr(=0)=0.6,\Pr(=100)=0.4$, $p=0.5$ gives $\mathrm{VaR}_{0.5}(X)=\mathrm{VaR}_{0.5}(Y)=0$ but $\mathrm{VaR}_{0.5}(X+Y)=100\gt0$.

Example 1 TVaR from a CDF / LEV table ▾

$X$ has $F(2415)=0.85$, $E[X\wedge 2415]=925$, and $E[X]=1500$. Find $\mathrm{TVaR}_{0.85}(X)$.

$\mathrm{VaR}_{0.85}(X)=F^{-1}(0.85)=2415$. Use the mean-excess form:

$$\mathrm{TVaR}_{0.85}(X)=2415+\frac{1500-925}{1-0.85}=2415+\frac{575}{0.15}=2415+3833.33.$$
Answer
$\mathrm{TVaR}_{0.85}(X)=6248.33$.
Example 2 Lognormal TVaR closed form ▾

$X\sim$ Lognormal$(\mu=2,\sigma^2=9)$, so $\sigma=3$. Find $\mathrm{TVaR}_{0.85}(X)$.

$E[X]=e^{2+9/2}=e^{6.5}=665.142$; $z_{0.85}=\Phi^{-1}(0.85)=1.03643$.

$$\mathrm{TVaR}_{0.85}=665.142\cdot\frac{\Phi(3-1.03643)}{1-0.85}=665.142\cdot\frac{\Phi(1.96357)}{0.15}=665.142\cdot\frac{0.97521}{0.15}.$$
Answer
$\mathrm{TVaR}_{0.85}(X)=4324.35$.

6. Coverage Modifications

Notation

Ordinary deductible $d$, policy limit (max payment) $u$, max covered loss $m=\dfrac{u}{\alpha}+d$ (so $m=d+u$ when $\alpha=1$), coinsurance $\alpha$, inflation factor $1+r$.

Per-loss payment (cost per loss), ground-up loss $X$

Ordinary deductible: $Y^L=(X-d)_+$, so $E[Y^L]=E[X]-E[X\wedge d]$.

Increased Limit Factor (ratio of LEVs) $$\mathrm{ILF}(u)=\frac{E[X\wedge u]}{E[X\wedge b]}\qquad(b=\text{original limit},\;u=\text{increased limit}).$$
Full structure (coinsurance, deductible, max covered loss $m$) $$Y^L=\alpha\big[(X\wedge m)-(X\wedge d)\big],\qquad E[Y^L]=\alpha\big(E[X\wedge m]-E[X\wedge d]\big)$$ With inflation $1+r$ applied to $X$ $$E[Y^L]=\alpha(1+r)\Big(E\big[X\wedge \tfrac{m}{1+r}\big]-E\big[X\wedge \tfrac{d}{1+r}\big]\Big)$$
The upper LEV is taken at $m$, never at the policy limit $u$

The maximum payment $u$ and the maximum covered loss $m$ coincide only when $\alpha=1$. With coinsurance, first solve $u=\alpha(m-d)$ for $m=\tfrac{u}{\alpha}+d$, then plug $m$ into the LEV. Plugging $u$ instead is the single most common coverage-modification error.

Per-payment (cost per payment) — condition on a payment ($X>d$)

$$E[Y^P]=\frac{E[Y^L]}{S(d)}=\frac{E[X]-E[X\wedge d]}{1-F(d)}$$

General: $\;E[Y^P]=\dfrac{E[Y^L]}{1-F(d)}$ (divide the per-loss result by $S(d)$).

Franchise deductible

Pays full $X$ if $X>d$ (not $X-d$): $\;E[Y^L]=E[X]-E[X\wedge d]+d\,S(d).$

Per payment: $\;E[Y^P]=E[Y^L_{\text{ordinary}}]/S(d)+d=e(d)+d.$ (Franchise = ordinary mean-excess shifted up by $d$.)

Loss Elimination Ratio

$$\mathrm{LER}(d)=\frac{E[X\wedge d]}{E[X]}=\frac{\text{losses eliminated by deductible}}{\text{total losses}}$$

Frequency under deductible

With deductible, expected #payments scales: $E[N^P]=v\,E[N]$ where $v=S(d)=$ Pr(loss exceeds $d$).

  • Poisson($\lambda$): payments $\sim$ Poisson($\lambda v$).
  • Neg.Bin($r,\beta$): payments $\sim$ Neg.Bin($r,\beta v$).
  • Binomial($m,q$): payments $\sim$ Binomial($m,qv$).

Reinsurance splits — cedant $Z_I$ vs reinsurer $Z_R$ (Topic 1)

$Y$ = the insurer's gross payment on a loss, $S$ = the aggregate over the period. Every treaty is the same two-layer split applied to a different quantity, and always $Z_I+Z_R=Y$.

Quota share — proportional; cedant keeps fraction $a$, reinsurer takes $1-a$ $$Z_I=aY,\qquad Z_R=(1-a)Y,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(Z_R)=(1-a)^2\mathrm{Var}(Y)$$ Surplus share — retained line $r$, insured value $V$, $k$-line treaty (fraction fixed per policy) $$\rho=\begin{cases}0,& V\le r\\[2pt]\min\!\Big(\dfrac{V-r}{V},\ \dfrac{kr}{V}\Big),& V>r\end{cases} \qquad Z_R=\rho\,Y,\quad Z_I=(1-\rho)Y,\quad \mathrm{Var}(Z_R)=\rho^2\mathrm{Var}(Y)$$ Excess of loss (per-risk / per-occurrence) — retention (attachment) $\delta$ $$Z_I=\min(Y,\delta)=Y\wedge\delta,\qquad Z_R=(Y-\delta)_+,\qquad E[Z_R]=E[Y]-E[Y\wedge\delta]$$ Aggregate XL = stop-loss on $S$, retention $\delta$ (see §4) $$Z_R=(S-\delta)_+,\qquad E[(S-\delta)_+]=E[S]-E[S\wedge\delta]$$ Layered XL — layer $k$ spans $(t_{k-1},t_k]$ and the reinsurer takes share $p_k$ of it $$Z_R=\sum_k p_k\big[(Y\wedge t_k)-(Y\wedge t_{k-1})\big]$$
Three reinsurance traps
  • Surplus share attaches to the insured value $V$, not to the loss. Fix $\rho$ from the policy first, then apply it to every claim on that policy whatever its size. Treating $r$ like an XL retention on the claim is the classic wrong answer.
  • Layered XL: apply each $p_k$ only to the loss inside that layer's band, then add. One percentage on the whole excess is wrong.
  • Unattributed percentage: "a 70% quota share" with no owner named means the reinsurer's share is 70%.

Impact on the cedant's numbers (the second half of the syllabus outcome): quota share scales premium and losses by the same factor $a$, so the net loss ratio is unchanged (before any ceding commission) while the variance falls by $a^2$; excess of loss censors the cedant's severity at $\delta$ — it strips the large-loss tail, so it cuts variance sharply but moves the mean only a little, and it costs correspondingly little premium. Keep ratemaking and reserving data consistently gross or net — never mix a gross triangle with net premium.

7. Estimation — Empirical, MLE, Moments & Percentiles

Empirical (complete, individual data, $n$ obs)

$$\hat F_n(x)=\frac{\#\{x_i\le x\}}{n},\qquad \hat S_n(x)=1-\hat F_n(x)$$

Empirical mean $=\bar x$; empirical variance $=\frac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2$.

Maximum Likelihood

$$L(\theta)=\prod_i (\text{contribution}_i),\qquad \ell=\ln L,\qquad \frac{\partial \ell}{\partial\theta}=0$$

Contribution by data type: complete $f(x_i)$; right-censored at $u$: $S(u)$; left-truncated at $d$: divide by $S(d)$, i.e. $f(x)/S(d)$ or $S(u)/S(d)$.

Key closed-form MLEs:

  • Exponential, complete: $\hat\theta=\bar x$.
  • Exponential w/ censoring/truncation: $\hat\theta=\dfrac{\text{total observed exposure}}{\#\text{uncensored}}=\dfrac{\sum_i\big(\min(x_i,u_i)-d_i\big)}{\#\text{uncensored}}$ — sum over all observations, censored ones included at their censoring point $u_i$.
  • Poisson: $\hat\lambda=\bar x$. Binomial (known $m$): $\hat q=\bar x/m$. Neg.Bin: $\hat\beta=\bar x/r$ (or method of moments for $r,\beta$).
  • Lognormal: $\hat\mu=\frac1n\sum\ln x_i$, $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(\ln x_i-\hat\mu)^2$.
Asymptotic variance

$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\approx \dfrac{1}{I(\theta)}$, where Fisher info $I(\theta)=-E\!\big[\tfrac{\partial^2\ell}{\partial\theta^2}\big]$ (or observed info $-\tfrac{\partial^2\ell}{\partial\theta^2}$). Delta method: $\mathrm{Var}(g(\hat\theta))\approx[g'(\theta)]^2\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)$.

Method of moments & percentile matching

Method of moments — one equation per unknown parameter $$E[X^k]\;\text{(as a function of the parameters)}\;=\;\frac1n\sum_i x_i^k,\qquad k=1,2,\dots$$

Use $k=1$ for one unknown, $k=1,2$ for two. Complete data only; simple but inefficient relative to MLE.

Percentile matching — fitted percentile $=$ smoothed empirical percentile $$\pi_p(\hat\theta)=\hat\pi_p,\qquad (n+1)p=j+h\;\;(j\in\mathbb{Z},\,0\le h<1),\qquad \hat\pi_p=(1-h)x_{(j)}+h\,x_{(j+1)}$$

Order the data $x_{(1)}\le\cdots\le x_{(n)}$ first. Use $(n+1)p$, not $np$, then interpolate between the two neighbouring order statistics.

Kaplan-Meier (product-limit) — survival w/ censoring — ASTAM background, not FAM

Removed from FAM effective November 2024 (the old mortality-estimation topic went when the exam dropped to 34 questions). Kaplan–Meier, Nelson–Aalen, Greenwood and the associated confidence intervals are now ASTAM. Read for intuition; don't drill.

At distinct death times $y_1\lt y_2<\cdots$, with $r_j$ at risk and $s_j$ deaths:

$$\hat S(t)=\prod_{j:\,y_j\le t}\Big(1-\frac{s_j}{r_j}\Big)$$

Risk set: $r_j = (\text{# with } d_i\lt y_j \le x_i)$ accounting for left-truncation $d_i$ and right-censor/death $x_i$.

Nelson-Aalen — cumulative hazard — ASTAM background, not FAM

$$\hat H(t)=\sum_{j:\,y_j\le t}\frac{s_j}{r_j},\qquad \hat S(t)=e^{-\hat H(t)}$$
Greenwood's formula (var of K-M) — ASTAM background, not FAM $$\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}[\hat S(t)]=\hat S(t)^2\sum_{j:\,y_j\le t}\frac{s_j}{r_j(r_j-s_j)}$$

N-A variance: $\;\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}[\hat H(t)]=\sum_{y_j\le t}\dfrac{s_j}{r_j^2}$ (or $\sum\frac{s_j(r_j-s_j)}{r_j^3}$).

Linear (log-transformed) CIs use these variances.

8. Credibility

Scope: FAM tests limited-fluctuation credibility only

Topic 4 (2.5–5%) is classical / limited-fluctuation credibility — full and partial credibility standards. Everything below headed Bühlmann, Bühlmann–Straub, Bayesian or empirical Bayes is greatest-accuracy credibility, which sits on ASTAM. It's kept here because it makes the classical case click, but memorize only the first block.

Limited-fluctuation (classical) — the FAM block

Full credibility standard for number of claims (for the mean within $\pm k$ with prob $p$):

$$\lambda_0=\Big(\frac{z_{(1+p)/2}}{k}\Big)^2$$
  • Claim count full-cred: $n_F=\lambda_0$ (Poisson) or $\lambda_0\cdot\dfrac{\sigma_N^2}{\mu_N}$ in general.
  • Aggregate/pure-premium, in expected claims (Poisson freq): $n_F=\lambda_0\Big(1+\dfrac{\sigma_X^2}{\mu_X^2}\Big)=\lambda_0\big(1+\mathrm{CV}_X^2\big)$; in exposures divide by mean frequency: $n_e=n_F/\mu_N$.
  • Partial credibility: $Z=\sqrt{n/n_F}\le1$.
  • Credibility premium: $P_c=Z\bar X+(1-Z)M$ ($M$ = manual/prior).

Bühlmann — ASTAM background, not FAM

For risk parameter $\Theta$:

  • Hypothetical mean $\mu(\theta)=E[X\mid\Theta=\theta]$; process variance $v(\theta)=\mathrm{Var}(X\mid\Theta=\theta)$.
$$\mu=E[\mu(\Theta)] \text{ (collective mean)}$$ $$v=E[v(\Theta)]=\text{Expected Process Variance (EPV)}$$ $$a=\mathrm{Var}[\mu(\Theta)]=\text{Variance of Hypothetical Means (VHM)}$$ $$k=\frac{v}{a},\qquad Z=\frac{n}{n+k},\qquad P_c=Z\bar X+(1-Z)\mu$$

Bühlmann-Straub (varying exposures $m_i$, total $m=\sum m_i$) — ASTAM background, not FAM

$$Z=\frac{m}{m+k},\qquad k=\frac{v}{a},\qquad \bar X=\frac{\sum m_i \bar X_i}{m}$$

Bayesian / structural — ASTAM background, not FAM

Unbiasedness: $E[P_c]=\mu$. Bühlmann is the best linear approximation to the Bayesian premium; for conjugate pairs (Poisson-Gamma, Binomial-Beta, Normal-Normal, Exp-Inv.Gamma) Bühlmann = Bayes exactly.

Empirical Bayes (nonparametric), $r$ groups, $n$ each $$\hat\mu=\bar X,\quad \hat v=\frac{1}{r(n-1)}\sum_i\sum_j (X_{ij}-\bar X_i)^2,$$ $$\hat a=\frac{1}{r-1}\sum_i(\bar X_i-\bar X)^2-\frac{\hat v}{n}$$

9. Ratemaking

Loss ratio (experience) method

$$\text{Loss Ratio}=\frac{\text{Losses}+\text{LAE}}{\text{Earned Premium}}$$ $$\text{Indicated rate change}=\frac{\text{Experience LR}+\text{Fixed expense ratio}}{1-\text{Variable expense \%}-\text{Profit \%}}-1$$

Target/permissible LR $=1-V-Q$ (V = variable expense, Q = profit & contingencies).

Pure premium method

$$\text{Pure Premium}=\frac{\text{Losses}+\text{LAE}}{\text{Exposures}}$$ $$\text{Gross (indicated) rate}=\frac{\text{Pure Premium}+\text{Fixed expense per exposure}}{1-V-Q}$$

Loss development & trend

Ultimate losses $=$ (reported)$\times$(LDF)$\times$(trend factor). Trend: $(1+t)^{n}$ over $n$ years to projected period.

On-leveling premium — parallelogram method

Adjust historical EP to current rate level by weighting rate-level periods by area (earned-exposure fraction).

$$\text{On-level factor}=\frac{\text{current rate level}}{\text{avg rate level for the period}}$$

Extension-of-exposures = re-rate each policy at current rates (exact).

Credibility in ratemaking

$$\text{Rate}=Z(\text{indicated})+(1-Z)(\text{current/complement})$$

10. Reserving (Loss Development)

Setup: cumulative claims triangle, accident years (AY) $i$, development lags $j$.

Age-to-age (link) factors — Chain Ladder

$$f_j=\frac{\sum_i C_{i,j+1}}{\sum_i C_{i,j}}\quad(\text{volume-weighted})$$

Cumulative development factor (CDF) to ultimate: $\;\text{CDF}_j=f_j f_{j+1}\cdots f_{n-1}$.

$$\hat C_{i,\text{ult}}=C_{i,\text{latest}}\times \text{CDF},\qquad \text{Reserve}_i=\hat C_{i,\text{ult}}-C_{i,\text{latest}}$$

Percent reported at lag $j$: $\;p_j=1/\text{CDF}_j$.

Bornhuetter-Ferguson

Uses an a-priori expected ultimate $\hat C_i^{(0)}$ (= premium $\times$ expected LR):

$$\hat C_i^{BF}=C_{i,\text{latest}}+\hat C_i^{(0)}\big(1-1/\text{CDF}_i\big)=C_{i,\text{latest}}+\hat C_i^{(0)}(1-p_i)$$ $$\text{Reserve}_i^{BF}=\hat C_i^{(0)}(1-p_i)$$

BF = credibility blend of chain-ladder (weight $p_i$) and expected-loss (weight $1-p_i$):

$$\hat C_i^{BF}=p_i\,\hat C_i^{CL}+(1-p_i)\,\hat C_i^{(0)}.$$

Expected loss ratio (expected claims) method

Pure expected: $\hat C_i^{(0)}=\text{Premium}_i\times \text{ELR}$; reserve $=\hat C_i^{(0)}-C_{i,\text{latest}}$ (ultimate ignores actual development to date — do not multiply by $(1-p_i)$; that is the BF reserve).

The three methods, side by side

All three set $\text{Reserve}_i=\hat C_{i,\text{ult}}-C_{i,\text{latest}}$; they differ only in how the ultimate is built.

  • Chain ladder — ultimate $=C_{i,\text{latest}}\times\text{CDF}_i$. Trusts the data completely; volatile for green years where $p_i$ is small.
  • Expected loss ratio — ultimate $=\text{Premium}_i\times\text{ELR}$. Ignores the data completely; stable but blind to actual experience.
  • Bornhuetter-Ferguson — the credibility blend of the two, with weight $p_i=1/\text{CDF}_i$ on chain ladder.

Check: $\;p_i R_i^{CL}+(1-p_i)R_i^{ELR}=R_i^{BF}$, which only balances when $R_i^{ELR}$ is ultimate minus paid.

11. Option Pricing

Notation

$S_0$ spot, $K$ strike, $r$ cont. risk-free, $T$ maturity, $h$ period length, $\sigma$ volatility, $C/P$ call/put prices, $\delta$ cont. dividend yield.

Exam scope: European options on a non-dividend-paying asset

Topic 6 asks for the binomial model and Black-Scholes on a European-style derivative on a single non-dividend-paying asset, put-call parity, and the delta hedge. That means $\delta=0$ everywhere — memorize the boxed $\delta=0$ forms. The general $\delta$ versions are shown beneath each one as background: no dividends, no American early exercise, and no Greeks beyond $\Delta$ appear in the FAM outcomes.

Put-Call Parity (European)

Memorize this — non-dividend stock $$C - P = S_0 - K e^{-rT}$$

Bounds: $\;\max(0,\,S_0-Ke^{-rT})\le C\le S_0$. Parity needs the same underlying, strike and expiry, and holds for European options only.

Background (beyond FAM scope): with a continuous dividend yield $\delta$, $\;C-P=S_0e^{-\delta T}-Ke^{-rT}$; with discrete dividends of present value $D$, $\;C-P=S_0-D-Ke^{-rT}$; for a currency option replace $\delta\to r_f$ and $S_0\to x_0$.

One-period Binomial

Up/down factors with volatility (forward tree, $\delta=0$): $u=e^{rh+\sigma\sqrt h}$, $d=e^{rh-\sigma\sqrt h}$. Many problems simply give you $u$ and $d$ (or $S_u,S_d$) instead.

Risk-neutral probability — memorize this $$p^\*=\frac{e^{rh}-d}{u-d}$$ Replicating portfolio ($\Delta$ shares $+$ $B$ in bonds) — this is the delta hedge $$\Delta=\frac{C_u-C_d}{S_0(u-d)},\qquad B=e^{-rh}\frac{uC_d-dC_u}{u-d}$$ $$C=\Delta S_0+B=e^{-rh}\big[p^\* C_u+(1-p^\*)C_d\big]$$

Multi-period: work backward node by node, discounting one period at a time. No-arbitrage requires $d\lt e^{rh}\lt u$.

Background (beyond FAM scope): with dividend yield $\delta$, $\;u=e^{(r-\delta)h+\sigma\sqrt h}$, $d=e^{(r-\delta)h-\sigma\sqrt h}$, $\;p^\*=\dfrac{e^{(r-\delta)h}-d}{u-d}$, $\;\Delta=e^{-\delta h}\dfrac{C_u-C_d}{S_0(u-d)}$. American options take $\max(\text{exercise},\text{hold})$ at each node — not tested on FAM.

Black-Scholes (European)

Memorize this — non-dividend stock $$C=S_0\Phi(d_1)-Ke^{-rT}\Phi(d_2),\qquad P=Ke^{-rT}\Phi(-d_2)-S_0\Phi(-d_1)$$ $$d_1=\frac{\ln(S_0/K)+\big(r+\tfrac12\sigma^2\big)T}{\sigma\sqrt T},\qquad d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt T$$ Delta hedge — the only Greek in the FAM outcomes $$\Delta_{\text{call}}=\Phi(d_1),\qquad \Delta_{\text{put}}=-\Phi(-d_1)=\Phi(d_1)-1$$

To hedge a written call on one share, hold $\Delta_{\text{call}}$ shares; the position costs $\Delta S_0$ and is financed by borrowing $\Delta S_0-C$.

Background (beyond FAM scope): with dividend yield $\delta$, replace $S_0\to S_0e^{-\delta T}$ in $C,P$ and $\Delta$, and use $d_1=\dfrac{\ln(S_0/K)+(r-\delta+\frac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T}$. Other Greeks: $\Gamma=\dfrac{\phi(d_1)}{S_0\sigma\sqrt T}$ (same for call and put), Vega $=S_0\phi(d_1)\sqrt T$.

Forwards / prepaid forwards — background, not a FAM outcome

Not named in the Topic 6 outcomes; useful only for reading parity as "buy the forward". Non-dividend asset: $\;F^P_{0,T}=S_0$ and $\;F_{0,T}=S_0e^{rT}$. With dividend yield $\delta$: $\;F^P_{0,T}=S_0e^{-\delta T}$, $\;F_{0,T}=S_0e^{(r-\delta)T}$; with discrete dividends $\;F^P_{0,T}=S_0-\mathrm{PV}(\text{divs})$.

Quick reference — key identities

Identities to have on instant recall
  • $E[X]=E[X\wedge d]+E[(X-d)_+]$
  • $\mathrm{Var}(S)=E[N]\mathrm{Var}(X)+\mathrm{Var}(N)E[X]^2$ (compound)
  • Compound Poisson: $\mathrm{Var}(S)=\lambda E[X^2]$
  • Normal approx: $\Pr(S\le s)\approx\Phi\!\big(\tfrac{s+0.5-E[S]}{\mathrm{SD}(S)}\big)$ (the $+0.5$ only if $S$ is discrete)
  • Coverage mods: max covered loss $m=\tfrac{u}{\alpha}+d$; $E[Y^L]=\alpha\big(E[X\wedge m]-E[X\wedge d]\big)$
  • XL reinsurance: $Z_R=(Y-\delta)_+$, $\;E[Z_R]=E[Y]-E[Y\wedge\delta]$; quota share $Z_R=(1-a)Y$
  • $Z_{classical}=\sqrt{n/n_F}$ (and $n_F=\lambda_0(1+\mathrm{CV}_X^2)$ is in claims, not exposures)
  • ELR reserve $=$ a-priori $-$ paid; BF reserve $=$ a-priori$\times(1-1/\text{CDF})$; CL reserve $=$ paid$\times(\text{CDF}-1)$
  • $C-P=S_0-Ke^{-rT}$ (non-dividend)
  • $p^\*=\dfrac{e^{rh}-d}{u-d}$ (non-dividend)
  • $Z_{B\ddot uhlmann}=\frac{n}{n+k},\;k=v/a=\mathrm{EPV}/\mathrm{VHM}$ (ASTAM background, not FAM)
  • $\mathrm{TVaR}_p=\mathrm{VaR}_p+\dfrac{E[X]-E[X\wedge \mathrm{VaR}_p]}{1-p}$ (VaR not coherent; TVaR coherent)
