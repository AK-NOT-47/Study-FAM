The phase structure (this is the key idea)

Every successful FAM plan moves through four phases. Don't skip ahead — each builds on the last. On an 8-week run the split is deliberately tilted away from reading and towards problems:

At ~224 hours (the midpoint of your 200–240 band): ≈78 h learn · ≈56 h drill · ≈56 h mix · ≈34 h mock 
Phase 1: LEARN        →  Phase 2: DRILL        →  Phase 3: MIX         →  Phase 4: MOCK
(read + worked egs)      (topic-by-topic           (mixed timed sets,      (full 34Q timed
                          problem sets)             both halves)            exams, review)
~35% of time             ~25%                      ~25%                    ~15%

Phases 1 and 2 are interleaved daily inside Weeks 1–6 — you never read a whole topic before touching a problem. Phase 3 owns Week 7 and Phase 4 owns Week 8.

Problems are where the learning happens

The mistake almost everyone makes: spending 90% of their time in Phase 1 (reading) and running out of time for Phases 2–4 (problems). Problems are where the learning actually happens. Start doing problems within the first week.

Your 8-week plan — anchored to Friday 23 October 2026

We lead with the Long-Term (FAM-L) half, because life contingencies is sequential — each topic depends on the previous one — so it rewards an unbroken run. Then we do Short-Term, which is modular and can absorb a bad day without derailing everything after it.

The weekly loop

Each week: read the listed topic page(s) → work all the worked examples by hand → do the self-check → then do practice problems on that topic → update the Progress Tracker page.

Two things this plan deliberately does not schedule
  • Multiple-state & multiple-decrement models — ALTAM material, not on FAM. The page L7 is kept as optional background only; skip it entirely until after 23 October.
  • Kaplan-Meier / Nelson-Aalen and mortality estimation — removed from FAM effective the November 2024 sitting. The surviving Topic 3 is Parametric Estimation of loss models (MLE, method of moments), which is what Week 6 covers.

Between them that is roughly a week of the eight you have. Spending it would cost you the exam.

Week 7 — Phase 3: Mix (Sat 10 Oct – Fri 16 Oct)

  • Daily mixed, timed problem sets pulling from both halves — see Worked Examples (Mixed Set) and Practice & Exam-Day Strategy.
  • Hammer your weak topics (your Progress Tracker tells you which — anything still 🔴 or 🟡).
  • Re-do every problem you've ever missed. Keep a running mistake log.
  • Memorize/internalize the FAM-L and FAM-S Master Formula Sheets in the Formula Sheets section.
  • End the week with your first full 34-question, 3.5-hour mock — you want the diagnosis while there is still time to act on it.

Week 8 — Phase 4: Mock & taper (Sat 17 Oct – exam day, Fri 23 Oct)

  • Two more full-length, 34-question, 3.5-hour timed mock exams under real conditions (your approved calculator(s) only — e.g. TI-30XS MultiView and/or BA II Plus, FAM tables only, no notes). With Week 7's mock that makes three.
  • Review every mock thoroughly — the review is worth more than the mock itself. Budget ~3 hours of review per 3.5-hour mock.
  • Last mock no later than Tuesday 20 October. Wed–Thu: formula sheets, mistake log, light review only.
  • Thursday 22 October: confirm your Prometric seat, ID and calculators; stop studying by early evening; rest before exam day. Do not cram new material in the last 3 days.

If you slip — and longer tracks for a future sitting

The 8-week plan has no slack, so protect the problem hours first: if a week runs over, skim the remaining reading and keep the problem sets, never the reverse. If you fall more than a week behind by 2 October, the honest move is to defer to the next sitting rather than sit under-prepared. On a longer runway the same four phases simply stretch: a 16-week standard track at ~18–20 hrs/week (comfortable and sustainable), or a 20–24-week extended track at ~12–15 hrs/week if you are studying alongside a busy job. The order never changes — FAM-L first, then FAM-S, then mix, then mock.

A good daily session (core block ≈3 hours)

  1. 5 min — review your mistake log from last session.
  2. 45 min — read/learn new material (work the examples by hand).
  3. 90 min — practice problems (timed once you're past Phase 1).
  4. 20 min — review what you got wrong; add to mistake log.
  5. 5 min — update the Progress Tracker page.
On the 8-week track, make it ~4½ hours

The block above is 2 h 45 m. To hit the 25–30 hours a week this calendar needs, add a second ~90-minute problem block later in the day — that gives ~4¼–4½ hours across 6 study days, or ~27 hours a week. Note that the extra time goes to problems, not more reading.

Weekly rhythm

  • 6 study days, 1 rest day — with 8 weeks there is no room for two.
  • 1 day per week is "catch-up + weak-topic" — no new material, just shore up gaps.
  • End each week by glancing at the Progress Tracker: is any topic still red?

Readiness check — am I ready to sit?

Sit the exam when all of these are true:

Green-light checklist
  • You've worked through every topic note and its self-checks.
  • You score consistently 70%+ on mixed, timed problem sets.
  • You've completed at least 3 full mock exams at passing level under real conditions.
  • The FAM-L and FAM-S formula sheets hold no surprises — you can reproduce the key formulas from memory.
  • You're finishing mocks within the time limit with a few minutes to spare.

If you use Coaching Actuaries ADAPT, an Earned Level of ~6–7+ is a widely-used "ready" signal.
