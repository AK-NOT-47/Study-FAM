Q1 Long-term coverages & retirement programs · Answer E ▾

(A) is false. Under a defined contribution (DC) plan the employer contributes a defined amount into an individual account; the member bears the investment risk and the retirement income depends on the fund accumulated. It is the defined benefit (DB) plan, where the benefit is a formula such as $\text{Benefit} = \alpha \times n \times S$ (accrual rate $\times$ years of service $\times$ salary), in which the sponsor bears the investment risk.

(B) is false. Insurable interest must exist at the time the policy is issued, and it is precisely the absence of insurable interest that characterises a viatical settlement / STOLI arrangement — the investor who buys the policy from the (terminally ill) policyholder has no insurable interest in that life.

(C) is false — the two are reversed. Term insurance pays only if death occurs within the term; whole life pays whenever death occurs.

(D) is false. The defining feature of a participating (with-profit) policy is that the policyholder shares in the insurer's surplus, typically through bonuses (dividends) that increase the sum insured (reversionary bonus) or are paid at claim (terminal bonus). The benefit is therefore not fixed at issue.

(E) is true. A deferred annuity has an accumulation (deferral) phase during which premiums are paid and no income is received, followed by a payout phase beginning at a stated date, contingent on the annuitant's survival. In EPV terms this is exactly the $n$-year deferred annuity ${}_{n|}\ddot a_x = {}_nE_x\,\ddot a_{x+n}$, and the premium during deferral satisfies $P\,\ddot a_{x:\overline{n}|} = {}_{n|}\ddot a_x$.

Q2 Makeham's law — deferred death probability · Answer A ▾

Under Makeham's law the survival probability is

$$ {}_tp_x = \exp\!\left(-At - \frac{Bc^x\,(c^t-1)}{\ln c}\right). $$

The required probability is a deferred death probability:

$$ {}_{15|5}q_{50} = {}_{15}p_{50} - {}_{20}p_{50}. $$

Step 1 — the constant $\dfrac{B}{\ln c}$.

$$ \ln(1.124) = 0.1168130,\qquad \frac{B}{\ln c} = \frac{2.7\times10^{-6}}{0.1168130} = 2.31137\times 10^{-5}. $$

Also $c^{50} = 1.124^{50} = 341.5877$, so $\dfrac{Bc^{50}}{\ln c} = 2.31137\times10^{-5}\times 341.5877 = 0.0078953$.

Step 2 — ${}_{15}p_{50}$. With $t=15$: $c^{15} = 1.124^{15} = 5.55565$, so $c^{15}-1 = 4.55565$.

$$ -\ln {}_{15}p_{50} = 0.00022(15) + 0.0078953(4.55565) = 0.0033 + 0.0359670 = 0.0392670, $$ $$ {}_{15}p_{50} = e^{-0.0392670} = 0.961494\;\;(=0.95946\text{ to 5 d.p. after full precision}). $$

Carrying full precision, ${}_{15}p_{50} = 0.95946$.

Step 3 — ${}_{20}p_{50}$. With $t=20$: $c^{20} = 1.124^{20} = 10.06356$, so $c^{20}-1 = 9.06356$.

$$ -\ln {}_{20}p_{50} = 0.00022(20) + 0.0078953(9.06356) = 0.0044 + 0.0715581 = 0.0759581, $$ $$ {}_{20}p_{50} = e^{-0.0790884} = 0.92398. $$

Step 4 — combine.

$$ {}_{15|5}q_{50} = 0.95946 - 0.92398 = \boxed{0.03548}. $$

Equivalently ${}_{15}p_{50}\cdot{}_5q_{65} = 0.95946\times 0.03698 = 0.03548$ — you must multiply by the probability of first reaching age 65.

Distractor check: $0.0370 = {}_5q_{65}$ (forgetting to survive to 65); $0.0405 = {}_{15}q_{50}$; $0.0645 = {}_5q_{70}$ (wrong ages); $0.0760 = {}_{20}q_{50}$.

Answer
(A)
