Example 1 Severity: per-loss expected cost with deductible and limit ▾

Ground-up losses $X$ follow an exponential distribution with mean $\theta = 1000$. A policy applies an ordinary deductible of $d = 250$ and the maximum payment per loss is $u = 2000$ (so the maximum covered loss is $m = d + u = 2250$).

Calculate the expected payment per loss (averaging over all losses, including those below the deductible that pay $0$).

  • (A) 597
  • (B) 624
  • (C) 651
  • (D) 673
  • (E) 702

Solution. For an exponential with mean $\theta$, the limited expected value is

$$E[X \wedge a] = \theta\left(1 - e^{-a/\theta}\right).$$

The expected payment per loss with deductible $d$ and maximum covered loss $m = d+u$ is

$$E[X \wedge m] - E[X \wedge d].$$

With $\theta = 1000$, $d = 250$, $m = 2250$:

$$E[X \wedge 2250] = 1000\left(1 - e^{-2.25}\right) = 1000(1 - 0.105399) = 894.601,$$ $$E[X \wedge 250] = 1000\left(1 - e^{-0.25}\right) = 1000(1 - 0.778801) = 221.199.$$ $$E[\text{payment per loss}] = 894.601 - 221.199 = 673.40.$$

Memoryless cross-check: $e^{-d/\theta}\,\theta\left(1 - e^{-u/\theta}\right) = 0.778801 \times 1000 \times (1 - e^{-2}) = 0.778801 \times 864.665 = 673.40.\ \checkmark$

Answer
(D) 673.
Example 2 Aggregate: compound Poisson standard deviation ▾

The number of claims $N$ is Poisson with mean $\lambda = 3$. Individual claim sizes $X$ are i.i.d., independent of $N$, with $E[X] = 500$ and $E[X^2] = 750{,}000$.

Let $S = X_1 + \cdots + X_N$ be the aggregate loss. Calculate the standard deviation of $S$.

  • (A) 1225
  • (B) 1369
  • (C) 1500
  • (D) 1620
  • (E) 1732

Solution. For a compound Poisson aggregate, $\operatorname{Var}(S) = \lambda\, E[X^2]$.

$$\operatorname{Var}(S) = 3 \times 750{,}000 = 2{,}250{,}000.$$ $$\operatorname{SD}(S) = \sqrt{2{,}250{,}000} = 1500.$$

(General check: $\operatorname{Var}(S) = E[N]\operatorname{Var}(X) + \operatorname{Var}(N)E[X]^2$. Here $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 750{,}000 - 500^2 = 500{,}000$, and $\operatorname{Var}(N) = 3$, so $3(500{,}000) + 3(250{,}000) = 1{,}500{,}000 + 750{,}000 = 2{,}250{,}000.\ \checkmark$)

Answer
(C) 1500.
Example 3 Coverage modification: inflation with a deductible ▾

In year 1, ground-up loss severity $X$ is uniform on $[0, 5000]$, and a policy has an ordinary deductible of $500$.

In year 2, every loss is subject to 8% uniform inflation (multiplied by $1.08$), but the deductible remains $500$.

Calculate the percentage increase, from year 1 to year 2, in the expected payment per loss.

  • (A) 8.0%
  • (B) 8.9%
  • (C) 9.8%
  • (D) 10.5%
  • (E) 11.2%

Solution. For $X \sim U(0,b)$ with deductible $d \le b$, the expected payment per loss is

$$E[(X-d)_+] = \frac{(b-d)^2}{2b}.$$

Year 1: $b = 5000$, $d = 500$:

$$\frac{(5000-500)^2}{2(5000)} = \frac{4500^2}{10000} = \frac{20{,}250{,}000}{10000} = 2025.$$

Year 2: losses inflate to $U(0, 5400)$, deductible still $500$:

$$\frac{(5400-500)^2}{2(5400)} = \frac{4900^2}{10800} = \frac{24{,}010{,}000}{10800} = 2223.15.$$

Percentage increase:

$$\frac{2223.15 - 2025}{2025} = \frac{198.15}{2025} = 0.0978 = 9.8\%.$$
Answer
(C) 9.8%.
Example 4 Credibility: limited fluctuation (classical) ▾

For full credibility of aggregate losses you are given:

  • The standard requires observed aggregate losses to be within $5\%$ of expected with probability $0.90$.
  • Claim frequency is Poisson.
  • Claim severity has mean $200$ and standard deviation $400$.
  • Use the normal approximation with $z_{0.95} = 1.645$.

Calculate the number of expected claims required for full credibility.

  • (A) 1082
  • (B) 2706
  • (C) 4330
  • (D) 5413
  • (E) 6764

Solution. The classical full-credibility standard for aggregate losses, in expected number of claims, is

$$n_0 = \left(\frac{z_p}{r}\right)^2\left(1 + \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\mu_X^2}\right).$$

Frequency-only base:

$$\left(\frac{1.645}{0.05}\right)^2 = 32.9^2 = 1082.41.$$

Severity adjustment (coefficient of variation squared):

$$1 + \frac{\sigma_X^2}{\mu_X^2} = 1 + \frac{400^2}{200^2} = 1 + \frac{160{,}000}{40{,}000} = 1 + 4 = 5.$$ $$n_0 = 1082.41 \times 5 = 5412.05 \;\longrightarrow\; 5413 \text{ claims (round up: the standard must be met, so take the next whole claim).}$$
Answer
(D) 5413.
Example 5 Ratemaking: loss ratio method ▾

An insurer reviews a line of business:

  • Developed, trended ultimate losses and LAE: $2{,}950{,}000$.
  • Experience-period earned premium at current rates: $4{,}000{,}000$.
  • Variable expense ratio: $20\%$ of premium.
  • Fixed expense ratio: $6\%$ of premium.
  • Target profit and contingencies provision: $4\%$ of premium.

Using the loss ratio method with permissible loss ratio $= 1 - (\text{variable} + \text{fixed} + \text{profit})$, calculate the indicated rate change.

  • (A) +1.3%
  • (B) +3.9%
  • (C) +5.4%
  • (D) +7.1%
  • (E) +9.2%

Solution.

Permissible loss ratio (PLR):

$$\text{PLR} = 1 - (0.20 + 0.06 + 0.04) = 1 - 0.30 = 0.70.$$

Experience loss & LAE ratio:

$$\frac{2{,}950{,}000}{4{,}000{,}000} = 0.7375.$$

Indicated rate change:

$$\frac{\text{Loss Ratio}}{\text{PLR}} - 1 = \frac{0.7375}{0.70} - 1 = 1.05357 - 1 = 0.05357 = +5.4\%.$$
Answer
(C) +5.4%.
Example 6 Reserving: chain-ladder (loss development) ▾

Cumulative paid losses (in thousands):

Accident YearDev 12Dev 24Dev 36
2023100015001650
202412001800—
20251400——

Development is complete at 36 months. Use volume-weighted (all-year) average age-to-age factors.

Calculate the total estimated unpaid (reserve) for accident years 2024 and 2025 combined, in thousands.

  • (A) 910
  • (B) 1010
  • (C) 1090
  • (D) 1180
  • (E) 1290

Solution. Volume-weighted age-to-age factors.

$12 \to 24$ (use all years with both points, 2023 and 2024):

$$f_{12} = \frac{1500 + 1800}{1000 + 1200} = \frac{3300}{2200} = 1.500.$$

$24 \to 36$ (only 2023 has both points):

$$f_{24} = \frac{1650}{1500} = 1.100.$$

Project to ultimate.

AY2024 at Dev 24 $= 1800$:

$$\text{Ult}_{2024} = 1800 \times 1.100 = 1980, \qquad \text{Unpaid}_{2024} = 1980 - 1800 = 180.$$

AY2025 at Dev 12 $= 1400$:

$$\text{Ult}_{2025} = 1400 \times 1.500 \times 1.100 = 1400 \times 1.65 = 2310, \qquad \text{Unpaid}_{2025} = 2310 - 1400 = 910.$$

Total reserve (2024 + 2025):

$$180 + 910 = 1090 \text{ (thousands)}.$$
Answer
(C) 1090.
Example 7 Survival models: constant force, deferred mortality ▾

Mortality follows a constant force $\mu = 0.02$ at all ages.

Calculate $\,_{10|}q_{40}$, the probability that $(40)$ survives 10 years and then dies within the following year.

  • (A) 0.0156
  • (B) 0.0162
  • (C) 0.0175
  • (D) 0.0184
  • (E) 0.0198

Solution. With constant force $\mu = 0.02$, $\,_t p_x = e^{-\mu t}$ for every age.

$$\,_{10}p_{40} = e^{-0.2} = 0.818731,$$ $$q_{50} = 1 - e^{-0.02} = 1 - 0.980199 = 0.019801.$$ $$\,_{10|}q_{40} = \,_{10}p_{40}\cdot q_{50} = 0.818731 \times 0.019801 = 0.016212.$$
Answer
(B) 0.0162.
Example 8 Life insurance: whole life APV (recursion) ▾

For a fully discrete whole life insurance of $1000$ on $(x)$:

  • $i = 0.05$
  • $q_x = 0.010$
  • $A_{x+1} = 0.350$

Calculate $1000\,A_x$.

  • (A) 332
  • (B) 336
  • (C) 340
  • (D) 344
  • (E) 348

Solution. Recursion: $A_x = v\big(q_x + p_x\,A_{x+1}\big)$, with $v = 1/1.05 = 0.952381$.

$$A_x = 0.952381\big(0.010 + 0.990 \times 0.350\big) = 0.952381(0.010 + 0.34650) = 0.952381 \times 0.35650 = 0.339524.$$ $$1000\,A_x = 339.5 \approx 340.$$
Answer
(C) 340.
Example 9 Annuities: temporary life annuity-due ▾

For a life aged 65:

  • $i = 0.04$
  • $\ddot{a}_{65} = 13.550$
  • $\ddot{a}_{75} = 9.900$
  • $\,_{10}p_{65} = 0.750$

Calculate $\ddot{a}_{65:\overline{10}|}$, the APV of a 10-year temporary life annuity-due of $1$ per year on $(65)$.

  • (A) 8.10
  • (B) 8.34
  • (C) 8.53
  • (D) 8.92
  • (E) 9.18

Solution. Use the recursion $\ddot{a}_{65} = \ddot{a}_{65:\overline{10}|} + \,_{10}E_{65}\,\ddot{a}_{75}$, where $\,_{10}E_{65} = v^{10}\,_{10}p_{65}$.

$$v^{10} = 1.04^{-10} = 0.675564,$$ $$\,_{10}E_{65} = 0.675564 \times 0.750 = 0.506673.$$ $$\ddot{a}_{65:\overline{10}|} = 13.550 - 0.506673 \times 9.900 = 13.550 - 5.01606 = 8.534.$$
Answer
(C) 8.53.
Example 10 Premiums: fully discrete net premium ▾

For a fully discrete whole life insurance of $100{,}000$ on $(x)$:

  • $i = 0.05$
  • $A_x = 0.30$

Calculate the annual net (benefit) premium.

  • (A) 1837
  • (B) 1955
  • (C) 2041
  • (D) 2143
  • (E) 2250

Solution. Fully discrete whole life net premium:

$$P = B\,\frac{A_x}{\ddot{a}_x}, \qquad \ddot{a}_x = \frac{1 - A_x}{d}, \qquad d = \frac{i}{1+i} = \frac{0.05}{1.05} = 0.047619.$$ $$\ddot{a}_x = \frac{1 - 0.30}{0.047619} = \frac{0.70}{0.047619} = 14.700.$$ $$P = 100{,}000 \times \frac{0.30}{14.700} = 100{,}000 \times 0.020408 = 2040.8 \approx 2041.$$

Cross-check: $P = B\,\dfrac{d\,A_x}{1 - A_x} = 100{,}000\cdot\dfrac{0.047619 \times 0.30}{0.70} = 2040.8.\ \checkmark$

Answer
(C) 2041.
Example 11 Reserves: fully discrete net premium reserve ▾

For a fully discrete whole life insurance of $1000$ on $(x)$:

  • $A_x = 0.25$
  • $A_{x+10} = 0.35$

Calculate the net premium reserve at time 10, $\,_{10}V$.

  • (A) 120
  • (B) 127
  • (C) 133
  • (D) 140
  • (E) 148

Solution. Whole life net premium reserve via the insurance form:

$$\,_{t}V = B\,\frac{A_{x+t} - A_x}{1 - A_x}.$$ $$\,_{10}V = 1000\cdot\frac{0.35 - 0.25}{1 - 0.25} = 1000\cdot\frac{0.10}{0.75} = 133.33.$$

(Equivalently $\,_{10}V = B\left(1 - \dfrac{\ddot{a}_{x+10}}{\ddot{a}_x}\right)$; the answer depends only on the given $A$ values, not on $i$.)

Answer
(C) 133.
Example 12 Mortality models: fractional ages under UDD ▾

You are given $q_{70} = 0.050$ and that deaths are uniformly distributed over each year of age (UDD).

Calculate $\,_{0.5}q_{70.25}$, the probability that a life aged $70.25$ dies within the next half year.

  • (A) 0.0244
  • (B) 0.0250
  • (C) 0.0253
  • (D) 0.0256
  • (E) 0.0263

Solution. Under UDD, $l_{x+t} = l_x\left(1 - t\,q_x\right)$ for $0 \le t \le 1$. Set $l_{70} = 1$:

$$l_{70.25} = 1 - 0.25(0.05) = 0.98750, \qquad l_{70.75} = 1 - 0.75(0.05) = 0.96250.$$ $$\,_{0.5}q_{70.25} = \frac{l_{70.25} - l_{70.75}}{l_{70.25}} = \frac{0.98750 - 0.96250}{0.98750} = \frac{0.02500}{0.98750} = 0.025316.$$

Equivalently $\,_{0.5}q_{70.25} = \dfrac{0.5\,q_{70}}{1 - 0.25\,q_{70}}$. The naive answer $0.5 \times 0.05 = 0.0250$ (option B) is wrong because the life has already survived a quarter of the year, so the denominator is $l_{70.25}$, not $l_{70}$.

Answer
(C) 0.0253.
Example 13 Reinsurance: per-risk excess of loss allocation ▾

An insurer buys per-risk excess-of-loss reinsurance covering the layer 1000 excess of 500: for each individual loss, the reinsurer pays the amount above a retention of $500$, up to a maximum reinsurance payment of $1000$ per loss.

Five losses occur during the year:

Loss12345
Amount300750140020003200

Calculate the total amount paid by the reinsurer.

  • (A) 1750
  • (B) 3150
  • (C) 4500
  • (D) 5350
  • (E) 7650

Solution. Per loss $X$, the reinsurer's payment is

$$R(X) = \min\big((X - 500)_+,\; 1000\big),$$

i.e. nothing below the retention $500$, then the excess, capped once the loss reaches $500 + 1000 = 1500$.

Reinsurer pays $0 + 250 + 900 + 1000 + 1000 = 3150$; the insurer retains $7650 - 3150 = 4500$ (choice C — read the question carefully).

Watch the two traps. Forgetting the cap gives $5350$ (D). Reading "1000" as a maximum covered loss instead of a layer limit gives $\min(X,1000) - \min(X,500)$, i.e. $1750$ (A) — once a loss exceeds $1500$ the insurer, not the reinsurer, is back on the hook for the excess.

Contrast — proportional (quota-share). Under a $25\%$ quota share the split ignores loss size entirely: the reinsurer pays $0.25 \times 7650 = 1912.5$ and the insurer $5737.5$, on every loss including the $300$ one.

Answer
(B) 3150.
Example 14 Option pricing: one-period binomial European call ▾

A non-dividend-paying stock has current price $S_0 = 100$. Over the next year its price moves to either $uS_0$ with $u = 1.25$ or $dS_0$ with $d = 0.80$. The annual effective risk-free rate is $r = 0.05$.

Calculate the price of a 1-year European call with strike $K = 105$.

  • (A) 9.52
  • (B) 10.58
  • (C) 11.11
  • (D) 12.50
  • (E) 13.23

Solution. The risk-neutral probability of an up move is

$$p^* = \frac{(1+r) - d}{u - d} = \frac{1.05 - 0.80}{1.25 - 0.80} = \frac{0.25}{0.45} = 0.555556.$$

Payoffs at time 1:

$$C_u = (125 - 105)_+ = 20, \qquad C_d = (80 - 105)_+ = 0.$$

Price = risk-neutral expected payoff, discounted at the risk-free rate:

$$C_0 = \frac{p^*C_u + (1-p^*)C_d}{1+r} = \frac{0.555556 \times 20}{1.05} = \frac{11.1111}{1.05} = 10.58.$$

Put-call parity cross-check. $C_0 - P_0 = S_0 - K v$ with $Kv = 105/1.05 = 100 = S_0$, so $P_0 = C_0 = 10.58$ here — the call and put are worth the same because the strike's present value equals the stock price.

Traps. Never use the real-world probability: $0.5 \times 20 / 1.05 = 9.52$ (A) is the classic wrong answer. Forgetting to discount gives $11.11$ (C).

Answer
(B) 10.58.
